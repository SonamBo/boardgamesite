Five in a Row (Gomoku): rules and strategy

Five in a Row is quick to learn and quick to lose. The key ideas are all about threats: making them, spotting them and stopping them.

Try them against the computer.

Play Five in a Row free

The rules

Players take turns placing a stone on an empty point of a 15×15 board. Gold goes first. The first to get five or more stones in an unbroken line, across, down or diagonally, wins.

How to win, in one line: block every open three straight away, and attack by making two threats at once, such as a four and an open three, so your opponent can only stop one.

Threes and fours

  • Open four: four in a row with both ends empty. It can’t be blocked: you win next turn.
  • Four: four in a row with one end open. Your opponent must block it now.
  • Open three: three in a row with both ends open. If it’s not blocked, it becomes an open four.

Block open threes immediately

Most beginner losses come from missing an open three. Each turn, scan for your opponent’s lines of three with space on both sides, and block one end.

Double threats win

A move that makes two threats at once, such as two open threes or a four and an open three, can’t be stopped with one stone. Look for spots where two of your lines cross.

Play compactly

Stones close together create many possible lines. Spread-out stones are easy to block one at a time.

The first-player advantage

On an open board, the first player has a strong advantage. That’s why you move first against the computer, and why expert tournaments use extra opening rules.

Opening tips

  • Start near the middle. Lines from the centre can run in every direction; a stone near the edge has room on one side only.
  • Play close to your stones. In the first moves, place each stone one or two points from your others, often diagonally. Diagonal shapes are harder for beginners to see.
  • Block from the right side. When you must block, choose the end that also builds one of your own lines.

Five in a Row, Gomoku, Renju and Caro

Gomoku is the traditional Japanese name for the game, usually played with Go stones on a Go board. Renju is a tournament version with extra restrictions on the first player, to balance the first-move advantage. Caro, popular in Vietnam, is a version where a five blocked at both ends doesn’t win. BoardGameSite uses the simple rules: any five or more in a line wins.

Five in a Row vs Four in a Row

In Four in a Row, discs drop to the bottom of a column, so you can only play the lowest space. In Five in a Row you can place a stone on any empty point. That freedom makes Five in a Row a game of open threats and fast attacks.

Five in a Row questions

What are the rules of Five in a Row?

Two players take turns placing a stone on an empty point. The first to make an unbroken line of five or more stones, across, down or diagonally, wins.

Is Gomoku the same as Five in a Row?

Yes. Gomoku is the Japanese name for Five in a Row. Some tournament versions add extra rules, but the basic game is the same.

Does the first player always win Gomoku?

On a 15×15 board with free rules, it has been shown that the first player can force a win with perfect play. People don’t play perfectly, so games are still competitive, but moving first is a real advantage.

What board size is used for Five in a Row?

BoardGameSite uses a 15×15 board, the standard size for Gomoku and Renju. It can also be played on a 19×19 Go board.

Can I play Five in a Row with a friend online?

What is an open three in Gomoku?

Three stones in a row with empty points at both ends. If it isn’t blocked, it can become an open four, which can’t be stopped.
