How to play Crazy Eights: rules and strategy

Crazy Eights is the card game that inspired Uno: match by suit or rank, play eights as wild cards, and be first to empty your hand. This guide covers the rules and the strategy that wins more hands.

The deal

Crazy Eights uses one standard deck of 52 cards. With two players each gets 7 cards; with three or four players, 5 each. The rest of the deck is placed face down as the draw pile, and the top card is turned face up to start the discard pile.

On BoardGameSite you can play one, two or three computer players at three levels, or a friend online by sending a link.

The rules

  1. Match the top card of the discard pile by suit (a heart on a heart) or by rank (a 9 on a 9).
  2. Eights are wild. You can play an 8 on anything, then choose the suit the next player must follow.
  3. Can’t play? Draw. Take one card from the draw pile. If it fits, you may play it straight away; if not, tap Pass and your turn ends.
  4. The first player to play their last card wins.

Strategy: save your eights

An eight gets you out of any situation, so treat it as your emergency card. Play it only when you have nothing else that fits, or when it’s your last card. Playing an eight early just to change suit usually wastes it.

Strategy: steer towards your long suit

When you do play an eight, name the suit you hold most of. You’ll be able to keep playing while your opponents draw. If you hold three spades and one heart, name spades.

Strategy: change suit by rank

You can change suit without an eight. Playing the 5 of spades on the 5 of hearts switches the game to spades. Use this to move away from a suit you’re short in, and towards one you’re long in. It also keeps your eights for later.

Strategy: read your opponents

When a player draws instead of playing, they probably have nothing in that suit and rank. Remember it. When an opponent is down to one or two cards, steer the game towards a suit they’ve shown they can’t follow, and hold back the rank that might let them change suit.

Strategy: get rid of high-risk cards

Cards of a suit nobody is playing can get stuck in your hand. If you have a choice of plays, shed the card that’s hardest to play later, like a lone card of a suit, and keep flexible cards that match several others in your hand.

Crazy Eights and Uno

Uno, created in 1971, is a version of Crazy Eights with its own deck. If you know Uno, the core is the same: match colour (here, suit) or number, use wild cards to change it, and race to empty your hand.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Playing an eight when another card would do.
  • Naming a suit you hold only one card of.
  • Ignoring which suits your opponents couldn’t play.
  • Holding on to a lone card of an unpopular suit until it’s too late.

Crazy Eights FAQ

Can I play an eight on an eight?

Yes. An eight can be played on anything, including another eight, and you choose the next suit.

Can I draw even if I have a card that fits?

Here you can draw only when nothing in your hand fits, except on Easy, where drawing is always allowed. If you have a choice of cards, though, you can always play another card and keep your eight.

What happens when the draw pile runs out?

The discard pile, except its top card, is shuffled to make a new draw pile.
