Peg Solitaire rules and solution: how to finish with one peg

Peg Solitaire has been played for over 300 years. Ending with a single peg is a real achievement, and ending with that peg in the centre is the classic perfect game.

Set up a board and try the plan as you read.

Play Peg Solitaire free

In short: jump a peg over its neighbour into an empty hole, straight across or up and down, and remove the peg you jumped. Keep going until no jumps are left. One peg left is a win, and one peg in the centre is perfect. Skip to the full solution.

The rules

The English board is a cross of 33 holes. You start with 32 pegs and the centre hole empty. A move is a jump: a peg jumps over a neighbouring peg into an empty hole directly beyond, left, right, up or down, and the peg it jumped over is removed. The game ends when no jumps are left. The fewer pegs remaining, the better; one is a win.

How good is my result?

  • 4 or more pegs: a good start. Keep practising.
  • 2 or 3 pegs: very good.
  • 1 peg: excellent. You’ve solved it.
  • 1 peg in the centre: perfect.

Strategy 1: think in arms

The board has a centre square and four arms. Clear one arm at a time instead of jumping wherever you can. Scattered pegs in different arms can’t reach each other later.

Strategy 2: never strand a peg

A peg alone at the tip of an arm, with no neighbour, can never move or be removed. Before every jump, check whether it leaves a peg isolated.

Strategy 3: the three-peg clear

A row of three pegs next to an empty hole can be cleared with a short pattern: jump the end peg in, then bring a peg from the side across to finish. Learning to remove pegs in threes like this is the key to cleaning up each arm.

Strategy 4: come back to the centre

As the board empties, steer your last pegs toward the middle. That keeps them close enough to jump each other, and sets up a centre finish.

Use undo

The best way to learn is to play to the end, then undo back to where things went wrong and try again. You can undo every jump on BoardGameSite.

English board vs European board

There are two common Peg Solitaire boards. The English board, the one on BoardGameSite, is a cross of 33 holes. The European (French) board adds four extra holes at the inside corners, making 37. The extra holes change everything: on the European board, starting with the centre empty, it’s impossible to finish with one peg in the centre. On the English board it can be done, which is why it’s the classic version.

A step-by-step plan

There’s no single sequence you need to memorise. This plan breaks the board into stages you can manage one at a time:

  1. Open toward the centre. All four first moves are the same puzzle turned around: a peg jumps into the centre from two holes away. Pick any one.
  2. Clear the arm you opened into. Work on that arm until only the pegs touching the centre square are left. Use the three-peg clear to remove pegs in groups.
  3. Work round the board. Move to the next arm, then the next, each time leaving the arm empty apart from the pegs next to the centre.
  4. Clean up the centre square. You should now have a small group of pegs around the middle. Count before every jump: the goal is to keep pairs of pegs next to each other so each can be jumped.
  5. Finish in the middle. With two pegs left, the last jump should land in the centre hole. If it doesn’t, undo a few moves and try a different order.

The full solution, move by move

Here is a complete solution for the English board that ends with one peg in the centre. To follow it, label the columns a to g from left to right and the rows 1 to 7 from bottom to top, like a chess board. The centre hole is d4, and the arms of the cross are the holes outside the middle three rows and columns.

Each move names the peg that jumps and the hole it lands in. b4 → d4 means the peg on b4 jumps right, over c4, into the empty centre, and the peg on c4 comes off the board.

That’s 31 jumps: one for each peg removed. Lost your place? Use undo in the game to step back, then pick up from the last move that matches your board.

Common mistakes

  • Jumping wherever a move is lit, with no plan for the arms.
  • Leaving a peg alone at the far end of an arm, where nothing can ever jump it.
  • Emptying the centre square too early, so the arms can’t connect.
  • Splitting the last pegs into two groups that can’t reach each other.

Some Peg Solitaire numbers

  • Every jump removes one peg, so finishing with one peg always takes exactly 31 jumps.
  • If a chain of jumps by the same peg counts as one move, the shortest solution to the English board takes 18 moves.
  • The puzzle has been played since at least the late 1600s, when it was a fashionable pastime in France.

Peg Solitaire questions

How do you play the peg board game?

Every peg board game works the same way: a peg jumps over a neighbouring peg into an empty hole, and the jumped peg is removed. You keep jumping until no moves are left, and the fewer pegs you leave, the better. The cross-shaped board with 33 holes is Peg Solitaire. The triangle board with 15 holes, often found on restaurant tables, uses the same jump.

What is the solution to Peg Solitaire?

Start with b4 → d4, c6 → c4 and e5 → c5, then follow the 31-move solution above. It clears the arms of the cross one by one and finishes with the last peg jumping into the centre.

What are the rules of Peg Solitaire?

Start with every hole filled except the centre. Jump one peg over a neighbouring peg into an empty hole directly beyond it, left, right, up or down. The jumped peg is removed. Keep jumping until no moves are left. One peg left is a win.

Can you jump diagonally in Peg Solitaire?

Not on the English board. Jumps go straight across or straight up and down only.

Is Peg Solitaire always solvable?

Yes, the standard English board starting with the centre empty can always be solved down to one peg in the centre. Mistakes along the way can make it impossible, which is why undo is so useful.

What is a good score in Peg Solitaire?

Two or three pegs left is very good. One peg is a solve, and one peg in the centre hole is a perfect game.

How many moves does it take to solve Peg Solitaire?

Always 31 jumps, because each jump removes one of the 32 pegs. Counting a chain of jumps by one peg as a single move, the fewest possible is 18.
