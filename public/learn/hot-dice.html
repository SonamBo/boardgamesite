Hot Dice (Farkle) rules, scoring and strategy

Hot Dice is all about risk. Knowing the odds of a farkle tells you when another roll is worth it.

Try the bank-points rule against the computer.

Play Hot Dice free

The rules

Roll six dice and set aside at least one scoring die. Then roll the rest to add more, or bank your points. If a roll has no scoring dice, you farkle and lose the turn’s points. If all six dice score, it’s hot dice: roll all six again. First to 4,000 wins.

Scoring: each 1 = 100, each 5 = 50, three of a kind = the number × 100 (three 1s = 1,000), and four, five or six of a kind double, triple or quadruple that. A 1–6 straight scores 1,500 and three pairs score 750, both only in one roll of six dice.

The farkle odds

  • 6 dice: about a 2% chance of farkling. Always roll.
  • 5 dice: about 8%.
  • 4 dice: about 16%.
  • 3 dice: about 28%.
  • 2 dice: about 44%.
  • 1 die: about 67%.

When to bank

A simple guide: with three or more dice left, keep rolling until you have about 350 to 400. With one or two dice left, bank anything over 300. Adjust for the score: push harder when you’re well behind or your opponent is close to 4,000.

Don’t always keep every scoring die

If you roll a 1 and a 5, keeping just the 1 and rolling five dice is often better than keeping both and rolling four. More dice means a lower chance to farkle and more chance of a big combination.

Scoring chart

  • Single 1: 100
  • Single 5: 50
  • Three 1s: 1,000
  • Three 2s / 3s / 4s / 5s / 6s: 200 / 300 / 400 / 500 / 600
  • Four of a kind: double the three-of-a-kind score
  • Five of a kind: triple it
  • Six of a kind: four times it
  • Straight 1–6: 1,500 (six dice, one roll)
  • Three pairs: 750 (six dice, one roll)

Dice only score in the roll they came up in. A 5 you rolled earlier and a pair of 5s you roll now don’t make three of a kind.

A sample turn

You roll six dice: 1, 3, 3, 4, 5, 6. You could keep the 1 and the 5 for 150 and roll four dice. Or keep just the 1 for 100 and roll five. Five dice farkle only about 8% of the time, against about 16% for four, so keeping just the 1 is usually better. Say you roll 5, 5, 5, 2, 4: three 5s add 500, making 600 with two dice left. Two dice farkle about 44% of the time, so with 600 banked, you should stop.

Hot Dice vs Farkle

They’re the same family of game. Farkle is the best-known name; Hot Dice is the name for the moment all six dice score and you roll them all again. House rules vary on scoring and the winning total. BoardGameSite uses the scores above and plays to 4,000.

Hot Dice and Farkle questions

What does “hot dice” mean?

Hot dice is when every one of the six dice has scored in a turn. You keep your points and roll all six dice again, adding to the same turn’s total.

What is a farkle?

A farkle is a roll with no scoring dice: no 1s, no 5s and no combinations. You lose all the points from that turn, and play passes on.

What are the odds of farkling?

About 2% with six dice, 8% with five, 16% with four, 28% with three, 44% with two and 67% with one.

When should you bank in Farkle?

A good rule is to keep rolling with three or more dice until you have 350 to 400 points, and to bank anything over 300 with one or two dice left. Take more risk when you’re well behind.

What scores the most in Farkle?

Six of a kind: six 1s score 4,000 here, enough to win the game in one roll.
