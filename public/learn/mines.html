The rules

The field is a grid of covered squares, and some hide mines. Uncover a square and it shows a number: how many of its eight neighbours, including diagonals, are mines. A blank square has none, and the game opens all the squares around it for you. Uncover every safe square to win. Uncover a mine and the game is over.

Your first tap is always safe. On BoardGameSite the mines are laid after your first tap, never on that square or the ones touching it.

Flags and quick clearing

When you know a square is a mine, flag it: long-press on a phone, right-click on a computer, or switch to Flag. Then tap a number whose mines are all flagged, and every other square around it opens at once. This is called chording, and it’s the secret to fast times.

Pattern 1: the lonely 1

A 1 with just one covered square next to it means that square is a mine. The same goes for any number that has exactly as many covered neighbours as its value.

Pattern 2: satisfied numbers

If a number already has that many flags around it, every other covered square around it is safe. Most clearing comes from these two patterns used again and again.

Pattern 3: 1-2-1 along a wall

Along a straight edge of uncovered squares, the numbers 1, 2, 1 in a row mean the squares opposite both 1s are mines and the square opposite the 2 is safe.

Pattern 4: 1-2-2-1

Along a straight edge, 1, 2, 2, 1 means the two squares opposite the 2s are mines and the squares opposite the 1s are safe.

Pattern 5: compare neighbours

Two numbers side by side share most of their neighbours. If a 1 and a 2 sit next to each other along an edge, the 2 has one more mine, and it must be in the squares the 2 can see but the 1 can’t. Thinking about what two numbers share solves many tricky spots.

When you have to guess

Mines are placed at random, so now and then the numbers can’t decide between two squares. Before guessing, uncover everything else you can. If you must guess, pick a square that would tell you the most if it’s safe, often one away from the tangle.

Reading the numbers: a worked example

Say you open a square showing 1, and it has three covered neighbours. Exactly one of those three is a mine, but you don’t know which yet. Now look at the number beside it. If that one is also a 1 and it can only see two of the same covered squares, the mine must be in one of those two, so the third square, the one only your first 1 can see, is safe. Most of Minesweeper is this kind of small comparison, repeated across the field.

Field sizes and mine counts

  • Easy: 9×9 with 10 mines. Good for learning the patterns.
  • Medium: 16×16 with 40 mines.
  • Hard: 30×16 with 99 mines, the classic expert field.

The counter shows mines left minus flags placed. Near the end, that number can solve a stuck position: if two possible patterns are left and only one uses the right number of mines, that’s the answer.

Pattern 6: the 1-1 from a wall

Two 1s side by side, starting at the edge of the field or against an opened area, share their mine. The covered square beside the second 1 that the first 1 can’t see is safe. This pattern appears constantly along the borders of a field.

A routine for fast clearing

  1. Open the first square anywhere. Corners and edges open smaller areas; the middle often opens a big patch.
  2. Flag every certain mine around the edge of the opening.
  3. Chord every satisfied number to open the squares around it.
  4. Look for 1-2-1 and 1-2-2-1 along straight edges.
  5. Only when nothing else works, compare neighbouring numbers or use the mine count.

Minesweeper questions

What do the numbers mean in Minesweeper?

Each number is how many mines touch that square, counting all eight neighbours, including the diagonals. A blank square has no mines next to it.

How do you play Minesweeper on a phone?

Tap to uncover a square and long-press to flag it. You can also switch to Flag mode with the Flag button. Tapping a number whose mines are all flagged opens the rest of its neighbours.

Is Minesweeper luck or skill?

Mostly skill. Nearly every square can be worked out from the numbers. Occasionally the numbers can’t decide between two squares and you have to guess, but good players guess far less often than beginners.

Is the first click in Minesweeper always safe?

On BoardGameSite, yes. Mines are placed after your first tap, never on that square or the squares touching it, so the first tap always opens an area.

What is chording in Minesweeper?

Chording means tapping a number that already has the right number of flags around it. Every other covered square around it opens at once, which saves many separate taps.
