How to play Snakes and Ladders: rules and history

Snakes and Ladders is one of the first board games many people ever play. Here are the rules we use, and the surprising story behind the game.

Start a game for two to four players.

Play Snakes and Ladders free

The rules

  1. Everyone starts on square 1.
  2. Take turns rolling the die and moving forward that many squares, following the numbers back and forth up the board.
  3. Land on the bottom of a ladder and climb to the top.
  4. Land on a snake’s head and slide down to its tail.
  5. Roll a 6 and you roll again.
  6. Land exactly on 100 to win. If a roll takes you past 100, count to 100 and bounce back the extra squares.

A game of pure chance

There are no choices to make in Snakes and Ladders. Every move is decided by the die, so a five-year-old has exactly the same chance as an adult. That’s why it’s such a good first game: it teaches counting, taking turns and winning or losing gracefully.

A long history

The game comes from ancient India, where it was played as Moksha Patam. The ladders stood for virtues and the snakes for vices, and reaching the final square represented liberation. It travelled to Britain in the 1800s and later to America, where it is often called Chutes and Ladders.

Playing with young children

  • Let them count the squares aloud as their counter moves.
  • Talk about the numbers on the die: which is the biggest, which is the smallest.
  • Celebrate the ladders and laugh at the snakes. It’s all part of the fun.

Popular house rules

Families have played Snakes and Ladders for generations, and many play it a little differently. Here are the common variations, if you’re playing at home:

  • Roll a 6 to start. Players wait off the board until they roll a 6.
  • Three 6s and you’re out. Rolling three 6s in a row sends you back to the start, or ends your turn.
  • Any finish counts. Instead of needing an exact roll, any roll that reaches or passes 100 wins. This makes games shorter.
  • Exact finish, no bounce. If your roll would take you past 100, you stay where you are.

On BoardGameSite, a 6 rolls again and you need an exact roll to land on 100, bouncing back if you go over.

How long does a game take?

With two players, a game usually takes about 10 to 15 minutes on a real board, and a few minutes online. Because every move is down to the dice, some games end in a handful of turns and others go on and on, which is part of the fun.

Snakes and Ladders vs Chutes and Ladders

They’re the same game. Chutes and Ladders is the name used for a popular American edition, with slides, or “chutes”, instead of snakes. The rules are essentially the same.

Snakes and Ladders questions

What are the rules of Snakes and Ladders?

Players take turns rolling a die and moving that many squares. Landing at the foot of a ladder takes you up it; landing on a snake’s head sends you down to its tail. The first player to reach square 100 wins.

Do you need an exact roll to win Snakes and Ladders?

In the classic rules, yes: you must land exactly on 100. On BoardGameSite, if your roll goes past 100 you bounce back the extra squares. Some families let any roll that reaches 100 win.

Is there any strategy in Snakes and Ladders?

No. Every move is decided by the die, so there are no choices to make. That makes it a fair game for children and adults playing together.

How many players can play Snakes and Ladders?

Two or more. On BoardGameSite you can play with two to four players on one device, or play against the computer.

Where did Snakes and Ladders come from?

Ancient India, where it was known as Moksha Patam and taught lessons about virtue and vice. It reached Britain in the 1800s.
