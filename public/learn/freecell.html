How to play FreeCell: strategy for solving any deal

FreeCell deals every card face up, so almost every game can be won with planning. This guide covers the layout and rules, how many cards you can move at once, and the habits that solve hard deals.

Try them on deal #1, a gentle start.

The layout

FreeCell uses one deck of 52 cards, all dealt face up into eight columns: the first four get seven cards, the other four get six. Above them are four free cells (single parking spaces) and four foundations, one per suit.

The rules

  1. Build the foundations up by suit, from Ace to King. Move all 52 cards there to win.
  2. Build down in the columns in alternating colours: a red 6 on a black 7, then a black 5 on the red 6.
  3. Use the free cells: any single card can be parked in an empty free cell, and moved out again later.
  4. Fill empty columns with any card or ordered run.
  5. Move one card at a time. The game moves an ordered run in one go when you have enough free cells and empty columns to do it card by card.

Deals are numbered 1 to 32000, the same as the classic Windows game, and cards that are safe to send to the foundations go up automatically.

How many cards can you move?

Moving a run really means moving it one card at a time through free spaces. With f empty free cells and e empty columns, you can move a run of up to (f + 1) × 2e cards, or (f + 1) × 2e−1 if you’re moving it into an empty column.

  • 4 free cells, no empty columns: up to 5 cards.
  • 2 free cells, 1 empty column: up to 6 cards.
  • 1 free cell, 2 empty columns: up to 8 cards.

That’s why empty columns are so valuable: each one doubles how much you can move.

Strategy: look before you move

Every card is face up, so the whole deal is a puzzle you can read. Before your first move, find all four Aces and the 2s. Count how many cards sit on top of each. Start with the ones that are easiest to dig out, and plan the first few moves before you make any of them.

Strategy: keep free cells empty

Free cells are temporary parking, not storage. Each card you leave there cuts how many cards you can move at once. A good habit: never fill the third free cell unless it wins you an Ace, a column or a big run.

Strategy: aim for an empty column

Look for the column with the fewest cards, or the one whose cards can all go somewhere useful. Emptying it early often unlocks the whole deal. Once you have an empty column, don’t fill it with a single card unless you have to: it’s worth more empty.

Strategy: build long runs, but not over low cards

Long ordered runs keep cards tidy and move as a block when you have space. But don’t build a long run on top of an Ace or 2 you’ll need soon: you’ll have to move the whole run to get it out.

Strategy: don’t rush the foundations

Sending low cards up is always good. Sending a 7 up early can hurt if you still need it to hold a red 6 in a column. The game only sends cards up automatically when it’s safe.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Filling all four free cells early and running out of room.
  • Putting a single card into an empty column without a plan.
  • Building runs on top of buried Aces.
  • Moving without looking for the Aces first.

FreeCell FAQ

Can every FreeCell game be won?

Almost. Of the original 32,000 numbered deals, only one, deal #11982, can’t be solved. The others can all be won with careful play.

What’s the difference between FreeCell and Klondike Solitaire?

In FreeCell every card is face up from the start and there is no deck to draw from, so it’s all planning and no luck. Klondike hides cards and deals from a deck.

How do I replay a deal?

Tap the Deal button beside the cards and enter the number you want, from 1 to 32000.
