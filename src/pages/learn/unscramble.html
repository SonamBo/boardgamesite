How to play Unscramble: solving word jumbles

Unscramble gives you ten jumbled words a round. This guide covers the rules and scoring, then the tricks that make words jump out: chunks, vowels, rare letters and smart hints.

The rules

  1. Each round has ten words with their letters mixed up. Choose short words (4–5 letters), medium (5–6) or long (7–8).
  2. Tap the letters in order, or type them, to build a word in the answer row. Tap a placed letter or press Backspace to take it back.
  3. Any real word counts, as long as it uses every letter. If the jumble is EILNST, both LISTEN and SILENT score.
  4. Stuck? Shuffle mixes the letters again, Hint places the next correct letter, and Skip moves on.

Each word is worth 10 points, minus 3 for every hint, down to a minimum of 2. Skipped words score nothing, so a round is out of 100.

Strategy: find the chunks

Don’t try to place all the letters at once. Look for groups that like to sit together and lock them in your head:

  • Pairs: TH, CH, SH, PH, WH, CK, QU, ST, SP, TR, BR, PL.
  • Endings: -ING, -ED, -ER, -LY, -EST, -TION, -NESS.
  • Beginnings: RE-, UN-, IN-, DIS-, PRE-.

With longer words, setting aside an ending first often leaves a short jumble that’s easy to solve. In NGIRTNAI, spot -ING and you’re left with R, T, N, A: TRAIN, so TRAINING.

Strategy: place the vowels

Most English words alternate between consonants and vowels, with a few consonant pairs in between. Count the vowels: a five-letter word with one vowel probably has a consonant cluster (STRAP, CRUST). With three vowels it may have two together (OCEAN, AUDIO).

Strategy: use the letter you can’t place

Rare letters such as J, Q, X, Z, K and V have few places they can go. Q is almost always followed by U. X often sits at the end (BOX, RELAX) or after E (EXIT, EXTRA). Start from the awkward letter and build the word around it.

Strategy: shuffle and say it aloud

Your brain gets stuck on the first order it sees. Shuffle shows the same letters in a new order, and the word often jumps out. Saying different letter combinations quietly to yourself helps too: you’ll often hear the word before you see it.

Strategy: use hints wisely

A hint fills in the first letter of the word, then the next. One hint costs 3 points but still leaves 7, much better than skipping. The first letter is usually the most useful clue, so if you’re stuck after a minute, take one hint and try again before taking another.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Trying to solve the whole word in one go instead of finding chunks.
  • Forgetting common endings like -ED and -ER.
  • Skipping a word when one hint would have solved it.
  • Assuming there’s only one right answer: any real word using all the letters counts.

Unscramble FAQ

What is an anagram?

A word made by rearranging the letters of another word, like LISTEN and SILENT. Unscrambling is finding an anagram that’s a real word.

Are plurals and past tenses accepted?

Yes, as long as they’re real English words that use all the letters.

How can I get better at unscrambling?

Practise spotting common pairs and endings, and play the longer words: they have more chunks to find, so they train the skill faster.
