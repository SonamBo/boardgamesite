How to play Spider Solitaire: winning strategy

Spider is a two-deck solitaire game where you build eight complete runs, King down to Ace, in suit. This guide covers the layout and rules, then how to build in suit, empty columns and time your deals.

The layout

Spider uses two decks, 104 cards. 54 are dealt into ten columns: the first four get six cards, the other six get five. Only the top card of each column is face up. The other 50 cards wait in the deck, to be dealt in five rows of ten.

Choose 1 suit (all spades) to learn, 2 suits for a real challenge, or 4 suits for experts.

The rules

  1. Build down by rank, in any suit: a 7 can go on any 8.
  2. Only same-suit runs move together. A run in order and all one suit (9♥ 8♥ 7♥) moves as a block; a mixed run doesn’t.
  3. Turn over hidden cards by moving away the cards on top of them.
  4. Fill empty columns with any card or same-suit run.
  5. Deal a new row when you’re stuck: one card on every column. Every column needs at least one card first.
  6. Complete a run from King to Ace in one suit and it’s cleared away. Clear all eight runs to win.

Strategy: build in suit whenever you can

A 7♥ on an 8♥ can later move with it; a 7♥ on an 8♠ can’t. When two moves are possible, choose the in-suit one. Off-suit building is still useful to free a card or uncover a hidden one, but it creates a joint that has to be untangled later.

Strategy: uncover hidden cards

Every face-down card you turn over is new information and new options. Prefer moves that reveal a card over moves that just rearrange face-up cards. Columns with the fewest hidden cards are the fastest to empty.

Strategy: empty a column

An empty column is the most powerful thing in Spider. Use it to park part of a mixed run, rearrange the cards underneath, and rebuild in suit. Once you have one, don’t drop a single card into it unless that card is a King or it leads somewhere: an empty column is worth more empty.

Strategy: deal at the right time

A new row covers every column, so make all your useful moves first. But don’t avoid dealing forever: once you’ve run out of constructive moves, the new cards are often exactly what you need. Before dealing, try to make sure no column is empty (you can’t deal otherwise) and that your best runs aren’t about to be buried by a card they can’t take.

Strategy: Kings are special

Nothing can be placed above a King, so it can only move into an empty column. A King that is the first card of a column is perfect: a run can be built down from it where it stands. A King turned up partway down a column is a problem, so plan to give it an empty column.

Strategy: use undo to explore

With two or four suits, try a line of play, and if it goes nowhere, undo and try another. It’s the best way to learn what works.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Building off-suit when an in-suit move was available.
  • Filling an empty column with a single low card.
  • Dealing a new row while useful moves are still left.
  • Starting with four suits before learning the game on one.

Spider Solitaire FAQ

How hard is four-suit Spider?

Very. Even expert players win only some four-suit games, while one-suit games can almost always be won.

Can I move a mixed-suit run?

Not as a block. You can move its cards one at a time, or move the same-suit part from the bottom.

Why can’t I deal a new row?

Every column needs at least one card before you deal. Move a card into each empty column first.
