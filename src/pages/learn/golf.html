How to play Golf Solitaire: lowering your score

Golf is a quick solitaire game scored like the sport: the fewer cards you leave, the better. This guide covers the layout and rules, then how to plan long runs and handle the Kings that block them.

The layout

Golf uses one deck of 52 cards. 35 cards are dealt face up into seven columns of five, so you can see everything from the start. One card is turned over to start the waste pile, and the other 16 form the deck.

The rules

  1. Play one up or one down. Move the bottom card of any column to the waste pile if it’s one rank higher or lower than the top waste card. Suits don’t matter.
  2. Nothing goes on a King. When a King is on the waste pile, you have to draw.
  3. Aces and Kings don’t connect. Only a 2 can go on an Ace.
  4. Draw when stuck. Tap the deck to turn over a new waste card. There’s only one pass through the deck.
  5. Your score is the number of cards left in the columns when you can’t go on. Lower is better, and 0 is a perfect round.

Strategy: plan your runs

Because all 35 cards are face up, you can plan exactly. Before you play a card, look at the run it starts. With a 7 on the waste and a 6, 5 and 8 available, going 6, 5 plays two cards and going 8 plays one. And remember each card you play reveals the card above it in that column, which might extend the run further.

Strategy: count the deck

You have only 16 draws. If you play about two cards per draw, you’ll clear around 32 of the 35 cards, which is a very good round. So every draw you make with a playable card still on the table is costly. Draw only when nothing fits, or when playing would cut off a much longer run later.

Strategy: save bridge cards

Some cards connect two possible runs. If a column holds a 9 and you expect to need it later to get from an 8 run to a 10 run, think twice before using it now. Planning where each run will end is the skill that separates good rounds from great ones.

Strategy: handle Kings carefully

A King on the waste stops everything, because nothing can follow it. Play Kings from the columns as the last card of a run (after a Queen), or just before you were going to draw anyway. Never play a King in the middle of a run that could have continued.

Strategy: dig out buried cards

Cards at the top of a column (the first ones dealt) can only be played once everything below them is gone. If a column is clogged with ranks that rarely come up, start working on it early.

What’s a good score?

Clearing every column is rare. Fewer than five cards left is a strong round, and under ten is respectable. Golf is also played as a set of nine “holes”, adding up the scores like a round of golf.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Playing the first card that fits instead of the longest run.
  • Putting a King on the waste in the middle of a run.
  • Thinking Aces and Kings connect: in Golf they don’t.
  • Drawing while a playable card is still showing.

Golf Solitaire FAQ

Is every Golf deal winnable?

No. A perfect 0 is possible in only some deals, so judge yourself by your average score.

Why is it called Golf?

Because, as in golf, the aim is the lowest score, and players often add up nine deals as nine holes.

Can I go through the deck twice?

No. Each deck card is turned over once.
