How to play Word Search: finding words faster

Word Search hides themed words in a grid of letters. This guide covers the rules and the three levels, then the scanning tricks that help you find every word in seconds.

The rules

  1. Each puzzle has a theme, like Animals or Space, and a list of words hidden in a grid of letters.
  2. Words run in straight lines and never bend. They can cross and share letters.
  3. Drag across a word from its first letter to its last, or tap the first letter and then the last. It’s circled in the grid and crossed off the list.
  4. Find every word to finish the puzzle. Your time is saved.

Easy grids are 8×8, with words that read left to right or top to bottom. Medium grids are 10×10 and add diagonals. Hard grids are 12×12, and words can run in any of eight directions, including backwards.

Strategy: hunt for rare letters

Letters like Q, Z, X, J, K and V are uncommon in the grid. If a word has one, find that letter first and check around it. In a list with ZEBRA, scanning for Z is much faster than scanning for E.

Strategy: look for double letters

Pairs such as LL, OO, EE, SS and TT stand out when you scan. A word like OTTER, BALLOON or COFFEE is easiest to find through its double. Remember that the pair can run in any direction the level allows.

Strategy: use the first two letters

Pick a word and scan the grid row by row for its first letter. Each time you find one, check the eight squares around it for the second letter. Only follow a direction where both match, then check the rest. This is far faster than reading every row looking for the whole word.

Strategy: scan in a pattern

Searching randomly makes you look at the same squares twice and miss others. Sweep the grid one row at a time, left to right, then (on Medium and Hard) one column at a time. On Hard, also check each row and column right to left: backwards words are easy to read past.

Strategy: work through the list in a good order

Long words are easiest to spot, and finding them circles many letters, which narrows the search for the rest. Words with rare letters come next. Save the short, common-letter words like SEA or TEN for last.

Strategy: when you’re stuck

Look at the letters that aren’t circled yet. Near the end of a puzzle, a remaining word must use some of them. Look away for a moment, then sweep the grid again: a fresh look often finds what you kept missing.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Reading every row hunting for the whole word instead of its first two letters.
  • Forgetting that words can run diagonally or backwards on harder levels.
  • Starting with the shortest words, which are the hardest to pin down.

Word Search FAQ

Are word searches good for your brain?

They’re a gentle workout for attention, pattern spotting and spelling, and a relaxing way to take a break.

Why can’t I find the last word?

It may run diagonally or backwards (on harder levels), or share letters with words you’ve already circled. Check the letters that aren’t circled yet.

How is my time measured?

The clock starts with your first find and stops when the last word is circled. Your best time for each level is saved.
