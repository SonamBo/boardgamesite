How to play Ship, Captain and Crew

Ship, Captain and Crew is an old sailors’ dice game: roll a 6, a 5 and a 4 in order, then load the best cargo you can. This guide covers the rules, the odds and when to re-roll.

Play a game to three rounds.

What you need and who goes first

Ship, Captain and Crew (also called Ship of Fools, or Six, Five, Four) uses five dice. On BoardGameSite you play the computer or a friend on one device. You roll first in the first round, and the first turn then alternates from round to round.

The rules

  1. Roll all five dice. You get up to three rolls a turn.
  2. Find your ship, captain and crew, in order: a 6 (the ship), then a 5 (the captain), then a 4 (the crew). A 5 only counts once you have the 6, and a 4 only once you have the 5. If 6, 5 and 4 come up together, they all count at once.
  3. Set them aside as they appear, and roll the rest.
  4. Load the cargo. Once you have 6, 5 and 4, the other two dice are your cargo, scoring their total from 2 to 12. If you have rolls left, you may roll the cargo again, or keep it.
  5. No crew, no cargo. If you don’t have all three by the end of your turn, you score nothing.

Each round, both players take one turn and the higher cargo wins. The first to win three rounds wins the game.

How likely is a full crew?

With three rolls, you complete the ship, captain and crew in roughly half of all turns, so expect plenty of rounds where someone scores nothing. That’s why going second can be such an advantage: you know whether you need a cargo at all.

Strategy: when to re-roll the cargo

Two dice average 7. So with rolls left:

  • Cargo of 6 or less: re-roll. On average you’ll do better.
  • Cargo of 7: it’s average. Re-roll with two rolls left if you need to beat something; otherwise keep it.
  • Cargo of 8 or 9: usually keep it, unless you’re going second and it isn’t enough to win.
  • Cargo of 10 or more: keep it. Only 6 of the 36 two-dice rolls do better than 9.

Strategy: going second

If your opponent has already played, you know the target. Stop the moment you beat it. If you haven’t beaten it, keep rolling with whatever rolls you have left, even with a decent cargo: an 8 that loses is worth nothing. And if your opponent got no crew, any cargo at all wins, so keep the first one you get.

Strategy: going first

Going first, you don’t know the target, so play the odds: keep 8 or more, and re-roll lower cargoes when you have rolls to spare.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Counting a 5 or a 4 before the 6 has been rolled.
  • Keeping a low cargo with two rolls left.
  • Going second and stopping on a cargo that doesn’t beat your opponent’s.
  • Re-rolling a 10 or 11 out of greed.

Ship, Captain and Crew FAQ

Why must the 6, 5 and 4 come in order?

Because a ship needs a captain before it can take on crew. It’s what makes the game tense: rolling a 5 and a 4 without a 6 gives you nothing yet.

What happens if both players get no crew, or the same cargo?

The round is tied and played again. Nobody wins it.

Where does the game come from?

It’s an old tavern and sailors’ dice game, played in bars in America for generations, often with a small bet on each round.
