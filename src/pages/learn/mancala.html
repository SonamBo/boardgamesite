How to play Mancala: rules and winning strategy

Mancala rewards counting. Know exactly where your last seed lands and you can chain extra turns and set up captures.

The rules

Each player has six pits with four seeds each and a store on their right. On your turn, pick up all the seeds from one of your pits and sow them anticlockwise, one per pit, including your own store but skipping your opponent’s. If the last seed lands in your store, take another turn. If it lands in an empty pit on your side and the pit opposite has seeds, capture them all, plus your last seed. When one side is empty, the other player stores their remaining seeds. Most seeds wins.

Count to your store

A pit lands in your store when its seed count equals its distance from the store. The pit next to your store needs 1 seed, the one before needs 2, and so on. Look for these every turn.

Chain your extra turns

Play the extra-turn pits closest to your store first. Emptying a far pit first can change the count of the near ones and spoil the chain.

Set up captures

An empty pit on your side, facing a full pit, is a capture waiting for the right count. Equally, watch your own full pits: if your opponent can land in the empty pit opposite, they’ll take them.

Hoard on the right

Seeds in the pits near your store are easy to bank later and hard for your opponent to reach. Late in the game, a big pile there can win it.

A strong opening

As first player, sowing the third pit from the left (four seeds) lands in your store for an extra turn. Then sow the pit next to your store.

Defend your pits

Every turn, look at your opponent’s side before you move. For each empty pit they have, ask: can they land a last seed there, and what’s in my pit opposite? If a big pit of yours is under threat, you can sow it before they capture, or fill the empty pit opposite it by sowing seeds into it.

Count the endgame

The game ends as soon as one player’s six pits are empty, and the other player stores everything left on their side. Before you empty your last pit, count: will your opponent keep more seeds than you gain? Sometimes the winning move is to keep a seed on your side, forcing your opponent to keep playing into your pits.

Know when you’ve won

There are 48 seeds in the game. Once you have 25 in your store, you’ve won, whatever happens next. Counting toward 25 helps you decide when to play safe and when to take risks.

Kalah, Oware and other Mancala games

Mancala is a whole family of sowing games played for centuries across Africa, the Middle East and Asia. Kalah, the version on BoardGameSite, is the one most people in the US and Europe learn first. Oware (also called Awale), popular in West Africa, has no stores you sow into and captures pits holding two or three seeds. The counting skills you learn in Kalah carry over to all of them.

Mancala questions

How do you win at Mancala?

Finish with more seeds in your store than your opponent. You collect seeds by sowing into your store, by capturing, and by keeping the seeds on your side when the game ends. Extra turns and captures are the two biggest sources of points.

What happens when your last seed lands in your store?

You take another turn straight away. Chaining several extra turns in a row is one of the strongest things you can do.

How do captures work in Mancala?

If your last seed lands in an empty pit on your side, and the pit directly opposite has seeds, you capture them all, plus your last seed, and put them in your store.

Who goes first in Mancala, and does it matter?

Going first is an advantage in Kalah, because the first player can start with an extra turn. On BoardGameSite you choose whether you or the computer moves first.

How many seeds do you need to win Mancala?

With four seeds in each of the 12 pits, there are 48 seeds. Twenty-five in your store guarantees a win.
