How to solve the Tower of Hanoi

The Tower of Hanoi has a beautiful secret: one simple pattern solves it perfectly, with any number of discs. Once you see it, you can solve 8 discs without a mistake.

Start with 3 discs, then add more.

The rules

There are three pegs and a tower of discs on the left peg, largest at the bottom. Move the whole tower to the right-hand peg. Only one disc moves at a time, only the top disc of a peg can move, and a disc can never go on top of a smaller one.

The least number of moves

With n discs, the least number of moves possible is 2ⁿ − 1. Each extra disc doubles the work, plus one.

The 3-disc solution, step by step

Call the pegs A (left), B (middle) and C (right). The discs start on A, and the goal is C.

  1. Small disc A → C
  2. Middle disc A → B
  3. Small disc C → B
  4. Large disc A → C
  5. Small disc B → A
  6. Middle disc B → C
  7. Small disc A → C

Seven moves, the least possible. Notice the small disc moves on every other turn: that’s the pattern below in action.

The idea behind it

To move a tower of 4 discs, you have to move the biggest disc at some point. It can only move when the other 3 are all stacked on the third peg. So: move the top 3 out of the way, move the big disc, then move the 3 back on top of it. Moving 3 discs works the same way with 2, and so on down. That’s why the count doubles with every disc.

The pattern you can actually use

  1. The smallest disc moves every other turn, always going around the pegs in the same direction.
  2. Which direction? With an even number of discs, the smallest disc goes left peg → middle → right → left, and so on. With an odd number, it goes left → right → middle → left.
  3. On the turns in between, make the only legal move that doesn’t touch the smallest disc. There’s always exactly one.

Follow these two alternating steps and the tower moves itself, in the fewest moves possible.

The legend

The puzzle was invented by the French mathematician Édouard Lucas in 1883. He sold it with a story about a temple where priests move 64 golden discs, and when they finish, the world will end. At one move per second, 64 discs would take about 585 billion years, so there’s no rush.

Tower of Hanoi questions

What is the least number of moves for the Tower of Hanoi?

2ⁿ − 1, where n is the number of discs: 7 moves for 3 discs, 15 for 4, 31 for 5 and 63 for 6.

How do you solve the Tower of Hanoi with 4 discs?

Move the top 3 discs to the middle peg (7 moves), move the largest disc to the right peg (1 move), then move the 3 discs on top of it (7 moves). That’s 15 moves. With an even number of discs, the smallest disc cycles left → middle → right.

Can the Tower of Hanoi be solved in fewer moves?

No. The largest disc has to move at least once, and every smaller tower has to be moved out of its way and back, so 2ⁿ − 1 is the minimum.

How long would 64 discs take?

About 585 billion years at one move per second: 2⁶⁴ − 1 is more than 18 quintillion moves.
